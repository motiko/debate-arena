# The Verdict decides whether the Motion stands

Debate Arena aims to be a truth-seeking platform, so the Verdict answers "given the arguments and evidence in this Debate, does the Motion stand?" rather than the competitive-debate question "which Side debated better?". For means the Motion stands, Against that it falls. The Judge decides from the transcript, and uses its own knowledge only to check factual claims, never to bring its own view of the Motion: otherwise one model's priors would decide every Debate and the speeches would not matter.

The Rubric Scores keep measuring how each Side argued. They are a separate question, so they may favour the Side that loses (a "low-point win"); when they do, the reasoning names the point that decides the Motion.

A Verdict also carries what a reader needs to weigh it:

- **Confidence**: clear, likely, or narrow ("too close to call"). A Verdict still always names a Side; narrow is how it says the Debate did not settle the question.
- **Crux**: the one question the disagreement comes down to.
- **Claim Checks**: up to six of the most important factual claims, marked supported, disputed or false with a note, from the Judge's own knowledge and without sources (and labelled as such). A false claim's argument counts as not established.
- **Ballots**: a Panel may seat three Judges from different model families, who answer independently and decide by majority; each Ballot and the split are shown. The default stays one Judge: at current prices three cost about four times as much (a custom Debate from 5 to 21 of the 24 daily Quota points, and an AI line-up over the day's Quota), so three are opt-in.

The Audience Result keeps measuring persuasion (ADR 0001) and says so: who moved the audience, not who is right. Swapping the Debaters between Sides and debating the Motion again is how the run-debate skill tests whether a result follows the Motion or the stronger model.

Verdicts from before this decision have no confidence and still read as "<Side> wins".

## Considered Options

- **Keep "who debated better"**: the established norm in competitive debate and easy to judge, but it measures technique, not whether the Motion holds, which is what the platform is for.
- **Let the Judge decide the Motion on its own view**: simple, but turns each Verdict into one model's opinion and makes the Debate irrelevant.
- **An "unresolved" outcome instead of narrow**: more literal, but every surface (feed, headline, stats) assumes a Side; narrow says the same thing without a third outcome.
- **Three Judges by default**: the most objective, but at today's prices it would use most of a Member's daily Quota on one Debate.
- **Fact-checking with web search**: sourced and stronger, but several times the cost per Judge and a new dependency; a later step.
